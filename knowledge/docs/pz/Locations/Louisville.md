Louisville , Kentucky, also known as “Gateway to the South,” stands as the largest city within Knox Country . Established in 1778, this metropolis comprises an array of neighborhoods, offering everything from tall high rises to dilapidated districts. As a major urban area, the city's vast commercial regions promise a massive abundance of supplies, but survivors should be wary of the high zombie population.

## Overview

Louisville, as a city, encompasses a variety of settings. From its downtown area replete with iconic landmarks to its industrious infrastructure, the city's history is palpable in its structures and avenues. Due to its size and population, Louisville hosts a significant number of public services. This includes two major hospitals , an expansive police station with an attached prison , and multiple large fire stations .

Many of Knox Country 's largest corporations have their head offices spread throughout Louisville. Including food chains Spiffo's and Jay's Chicken , fuel giants Fossoil and Gas 2 Go , and media such as LBMW and KnoxTalk Radio .

### Story

The outbreak of the Knox Event on July 6th brought swift military intervention. Anticipating the impending crisis, the US Military began erecting fences and setting up fortified border posts, sealing off the city and establishing the Knox Exclusion Zone. By July 9th (the game's default start date) accessing Louisville became near-impossible bar a few guarded checkpoints. The city was quarantined due to the threat of the Knox Infection . The situation deteriorated rapidly after the night of July 14th, and by July 16th Louisville was completely overwhelmed by the Knox Infection.

## Districts

Louisville has many districts that make up the vast expanse of the city. Everything from towering skyscrapers, to dense sprawling complexes of suburban housing, and to quaint farmlands can be found within the area on the north side of the fence .

Note that intra-city district boundaries are undefined as the world map only places labels over general areas.

### Audubon

The area Audubon seems to cover is mostly made up of many suburban houses. Notable named residential areas include Evergreen Hill , with points of interest being Evergreen Public School and Evergreen Hill Community Center . There is also a complex of luxurious mansions surrounding a small park.

### Commercial Area

This seems to refer to the commercial and retail area featuring the South Louisville mall , a GigaMart department store, some restaurants, and multiple strip malls.

### Downtown

Located in the northern part of the city, it serves as the heart of Louisville, a vibrant commercial and partly residential hub with many municipal buildings, multiple office towers, and much more buildings for retail, commercial, entertainment, dining, nightlife, and cultural events

### East Market District

Louisville's substantial population has spurred the development of a thriving commercial sector, particularly in proximity to major residential areas. Notable examples include the expansive Grand Ohio Mall , one of several large malls and shopping centers throughout the city. The area East Market District covers also seems to include multiple apartment buildings and many small rowhouses.

### Germantown

The area Germantown seems to cover is mostly made up of many rowhouses and small single-family detached homes densely-packed in a small area.

### Industrial Area

In the northwest corner of the city lies a large industrial and partly commercial and residential complex, which includes the Fossoil refinery , Gas 2 Go refinery ,  numerous factories, two warehouses, and the Ready Prep Army Issue Surplus . There is also a small rowhouse neighborhood here. The coverage of this area may also include Du Case Apartments and the Louisville Animal Shelter .

### Limerick

Located southwest of the city center, this area is home to the Louisville State University campus, a densely populated hub for students and staff. The campus features two primary dormitories that provide housing for students, alongside two main educational buildings equipped for a variety of academic programs. Administrative functions are managed from the dedicated staff office. Recreational and athletic activities are supported by a state-of-the-art sporting center, a professionally maintained running track, and a football field that hosts university matches and events.

Beyond the Louisville State University, the area Limerick seems to cover is mostly made up of neighborhoods of small single-family homes. Limerick may also cover Rely Manufacturing Co. , Scarlet Oak Distillery , and the northern end of the waterfront mansions.

### Parkwood

The area Parkwood seems to cover is almost entirely residential, containing many densely-packed small single-family homes. The crazy gun guy lives here.

### Wyandotte

Located in the city's southwest, just south of Louisville State University, near the exclusion zone fence. The area may be overlapping the Leafhill Heights neighborhood. Some notable areas include Leafhill Heights Offices with the AmeriGlobe Communications building next to the office, and the Leafhill Heights Elementary School , which is included within the neighborhood. The definition of Wyandotte may be stretched as far to include the St. Peregrin Hospital as well.

## Strategy

- Louisville is the most populated city in the game , having tens of thousands of zombies within. This is offset by the sheer size of the city, which makes most of the city relatively moderate when it comes to zombie density. However, downtown Louisville is utterly infested with thousands upon thousands of zombies. Having some levels in Nimble and combat skills can be a good idea before venturing here.
- Louisville has an extremely vast amount of loot, offering plenty of everything for the taking. This, along with the high zombie population and large size of the city, makes the city perfect for experienced players or for those seeking a more thrilling experience. This large amount of loot also includes tool and melee weapon spawns, making it easier to deal with the zombies here. The larger population is also a larger sample size to find weapons stuck on zombies. Some may find simply living in Louisville as a more attractive option instead of hauling all the loot out to another city or location.
- It is not possible to spawn in Louisville without mods. Always bring extra supplies for the journey.
- It is a good idea to have some degree of combat skills if one wishes to travel to Louisville. Barring that, most characters and builds will be effective in Louisville, and most general strategies to survive elsewhere will also apply to Louisville.

## Entering Louisville

There are only two roads leading into the panhandle portion of the map where Louisville and Valley Station lie. Traveling towards Louisville, will eventually find yourself at the Salt River just outside of West Point . The main Dixie Highway bridge across the Salt River will always be blocked with ruined cars, but this obstruction can easily circumvented by taking the rail bridge right beside it. Additionally, there is a smaller bridge just further upstream.

From Valley Station, there are only three roads leading into Louisville. However, all of the roads are guarded by military checkpoints are huge hotspots of zombies, with the Dixie Highway checkpoint being especially bad.

### Dixie Highway

The Dixie highway is the main highway that runs throughout the eastern side of the map. It is a single road that leads all the way to the border of Louisville, and the Army checkpoint. This is the most straightforward route into Louisville, featuring plenty of military loot at the Louisville border camp , which the highway leads to.

Just before the fence line, there is a pile of ruined vehicles that ends at the roadblocks just further ahead. You can easily just kill the few zombies here, drive around the pile of cars, and move the roadblocks aside.

However, inside the main army checkpoint and the campsites are an absurd amount of zombies, numbering in the thousands. Players can expect a siege that is several days or even weeks long in order to clear out this place.

### Louisville Road

The Louisville Rd checkpoint is a much quieter location for the player to enter Louisville, it can be accessed easily by turning off the Dixie highway towards Appletree  Lane just before it bends into the main entrance. Follow Bearcamp Rd east until you reach the intersection with Louisville Rd. Then head north until you reach the border fence. Welding tools or a sledgehammer will be needed to remove the fences, however.

Once the player breaks through the fence, they have direct access straight into southern Louisville, but will have less immediate access to loot. Some military loot can be found here.

### Ernest Cooper Road

The Ernest Cooper checkpoint is again quieter for the player to enter Louisville, but is harder to get to due to its distance from major roads. The player can access it by following the instructions for the Louisville Road checkpoint, but instead of turning, they continue down Bearcamp Rd. Keep traveling down Bearcamp Rd until you reach the intersection with Washington Rd. Turn to Washington Rd, then finally turn to Ernest Cooper Rd. Keep heading north until you reach the fence. Again, welding tools or a sledgehammer will be needed to remove the fences.

Once the player has removed the fence they can enter Louisville. Basic military loot can be found in the checkpoint. Continuing to travel north will bring them out into far southeast Louisville.

## Trivia

- Louisville is not available as a spawn location due to it being quarantined in lore until after the default start date.
- Despite Louisville canonically not falling to the zombies until July 14, the player can visit Louisville and still find it overrun with zombies by July 9. This is because the story is not actually reflected in real gameplay.
- The player can find many of the locations heard about in radio reports in Louisville, including the location of General John McGrew's speech at the border.
- In the 1990s, the population of Louisville was recorded at 269,063, as per Wikipedia .
